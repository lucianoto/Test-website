Planned structure

A working outline, so the shape of the page is visible while the content is still being prepared.

  • Part 1

    What it does, and why

    The mechanism in plain language, connected back to mucus clearance — what physical effect the device produces and what that accomplishes in the airway.

  • Part 2

    Know your parts

    A labelled diagram of every component, with what each one is called, what it does, and whether it is washable, disposable, or replaced on a schedule.

  • Part 3

    Assembly

    Step-by-step assembly with a check at the end so a wrongly seated part is caught before treatment rather than after.

  • Part 4

    Running a session

    Positioning, breathing technique, how long, how many cycles, and where the device sits in the daily order of treatments.

  • Part 5

    Settings and adjustment

    Any dials, resistance levels, pressures, or timings — what they mean, and who is allowed to change them.

  • Part 6

    Troubleshooting

    Common faults and what to do: nothing happening, weak output, leaks, unusual noise, alarms, and when to stop and call the clinic.

What is needed to finish this page

  • Device make and model — the exact name printed on the unit.
  • The manufacturer's instructions for use (IFU) — a PDF or a link. This is the authoritative source and everything here must agree with it.
  • Photographs of the device, ideally assembled and disassembled, or confirmation that we should draw diagrams instead.
  • Any local protocol your care team or programme uses that differs from the manufacturer's default.
Until this page is complete

Use the instruction booklet that came with your device, and ask your CF care team to watch you use it at least once. Technique errors are common, easy to correct in person, and quietly reduce how much benefit you get from every session.

Medical disclaimer. This guide is educational and does not replace advice from your cystic fibrosis care team, or the instructions supplied by the device manufacturer. Where this site and your manufacturer's instructions differ, follow the manufacturer. Always confirm changes to your treatment or cleaning routine with your clinician.
