About the GNuLab

The Georgetown Nanoscience and Microtechnology Laboratory (GNuLab) — also known as the Paranjape Lab — is directed by Makarand (Mak) Paranjape, Associate Professor of Physics. The laboratory engineers functional microscale sensors, non-invasive transdermal diagnostic patches, and biomedical microdevices.

GNuLab is a 3,300-square-foot shared-use clean room in the Department of Physics in Regents Hall, equipped with instrumentation for the fabrication and characterisation of materials and electronic devices. It is open to researchers across the Georgetown University Main Campus and Medical Center as well as to external collaborators, who are trained and supported in their work by Dr. Jasper Nijdam.

Projects in the lab are led by faculty research groups and carried out by postdoctoral fellows alongside graduate, undergraduate, and high-school students. Current work spans condensed matter and materials science — biodegradable polymers, two-dimensional nanomaterials, and atomic layer deposition of new materials — biomedical devices including glucose sensors, neural probes, and MRI contrast agents, and carbon nanotube sensors for gas monitoring and diabetes.

Medical disclaimer. This site is educational and does not replace advice from your cystic fibrosis care team. The device described here is a research prototype under development: it is not approved for clinical use, is not available to patients, and must not be used to diagnose or treat cystic fibrosis. Always confirm changes to your treatment with your care team.
