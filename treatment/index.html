Section 3

Treatment

This section is being written. It will cover what happens after a cystic fibrosis diagnosis — the treatments available, what daily life with them looks like, and where this device could fit alongside them if development succeeds.

Content to be written

Awaiting clinical content

Treatment guidance carries more risk than the rest of this site, so nothing here is drafted yet. It should be written against current CF care guidelines and reviewed by a clinician before publication.

Planned structure

A working outline, so the shape of the page is visible while the content is prepared.

  • Part 1

    The first weeks after diagnosis

    What happens once CF is confirmed: referral to a specialist centre, the team you will meet, and the tests that follow.

  • Part 2

    CFTR modulator therapies

    What they do, why eligibility depends on genotype, and what that means for people whose variants are not covered — see how CF is inherited.

  • Part 3

    Airway clearance and inhaled therapies

    The daily routine that keeps mucus moving, and why it continues even when modulator therapy is working well.

  • Part 4

    Nutrition and pancreatic care

    Enzyme replacement, fat-soluble vitamins, and the high-calorie diet most people with CF need.

  • Part 5

    Monitoring and infection management

    Routine review, sputum surveillance, and treating exacerbations early.

  • Part 6

    Where the patch could fit

    How transdermal sweat testing and drug delivery might sit alongside the treatments above, if development succeeds — see how the device works.

What is needed to finish this page

Until this page is complete

Treatment decisions belong with your CF care team. The Resources section lists specialist centres and patient organisations that can help you reach one.

Medical disclaimer. This site is educational and does not replace advice from your cystic fibrosis care team. The device described here is a research prototype under development: it is not approved for clinical use, is not available to patients, and must not be used to diagnose or treat cystic fibrosis. Always confirm changes to your treatment with your care team.
