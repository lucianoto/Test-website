Section 3

Regional Resources & Support After Diagnosis

A new CF diagnosis raises one question before all the others: who do we talk to now? Care is far better when it runs through a specialist centre and a community that has been here before. This page points to both, by region.

Before you go anywhere else

The most valuable single step after diagnosis is getting into the care of a specialist CF centre — a multidisciplinary team including a pulmonologist, dietitian, physiotherapist, nurse specialist, and social worker. Outcomes at specialist centres are consistently better than with general care, and the difference is not small. Everything on this page is in service of that one step.

India Region

Context

Cystic fibrosis was long assumed to be rare or absent in South Asian populations. That assumption has not held up: CF is present in India and the more pressing problem is under-diagnosis and delayed diagnosis rather than genuine rarity. The Cystic Fibrosis Trust of India estimates there may be up to 39,200 people with CF in the country.

Two things make diagnosis harder in India than in Europe or North America:

  • Symptom overlap. Chronic cough, recurrent chest infection, and poor weight gain are commonly attributed first to tuberculosis, recurrent pneumonia, or malnutrition — all of which are far more prevalent. CF is often considered only after those have been treated without success.
  • Mutation spectrum. The CFTR variants found in Indian patients differ from those common in European populations, and F508del accounts for a smaller share of alleles. Standard commercial genetic panels are built around European variants, so a negative panel does not rule out CF — extended sequencing may be needed. Sweat chloride testing remains central.

Newborn screening for CF is not routine nationally, so most diagnoses follow clinical suspicion. If you are pushing for an answer, asking specifically for a sweat chloride test at a centre that performs them regularly is usually the most productive request.

Support and advocacy

  • Cystic Fibrosis Trust of India The main national patient organisation. Provides education on CF, patient registration and peer connection, guidance on sourcing medication and financial grants, information on government and private care centres, and advocacy with pharmaceutical companies, diagnostic labs, and device manufacturers. Contact: care@cysticfibrosisindia.org

Specialist care centres

CF care in India is concentrated in a small number of tertiary institutions with established paediatric pulmonology or gastroenterology programmes. Centres that have published CF patient cohorts and are recognised in the Indian CF literature include AIIMS New Delhi, PGIMER Chandigarh, and Christian Medical College, Vellore.

Verify — clinic details

Individual CF clinic days, referral routes, and contact numbers change and are not reliably published online. Rather than listing numbers here that may be wrong, contact the Cystic Fibrosis Trust of India above — they maintain current information on government centres of excellence and private care centres, and can route you to the nearest one.

If you want named clinics with direct contact details on this page, that list needs to be sourced and confirmed before publishing.

Clinical detail: practical considerations for CF care in India
  • Salt depletion. Hot climates substantially increase salt loss through sweat in CF. Sodium supplementation and attention to hydration are more important here than in temperate settings, particularly in infants and during summer months.
  • Water quality for equipment. Rinsing device parts under the tap reintroduces waterborne organisms — including non-tuberculous mycobacteria — that matter a great deal in CF. Where sterile water is impractical for routine rinsing, boiled-and-cooled water is the accessible substitute. This is a real constraint, not a theoretical one, and is worth discussing explicitly with your care team.
  • Access to CFTR modulators. Availability and affordability are significant barriers, and eligibility is genotype-dependent — which interacts badly with the mutation-spectrum issue above. The Trust of India works on access and affordability and is the right first point of contact on this.
  • Pancreatic enzyme replacement and nutrition. Adequate enzyme dosing and a high-calorie diet are foundational and often under-prescribed where CF is not the primary working diagnosis.

China Region

Context

CF has historically been considered very rare in Han Chinese populations, and for a long time was barely on the diagnostic map. That is changing: it is now described in the literature as a rare disease emerging in China — reflecting improved recognition and genetic testing rather than a change in the disease itself.

The diagnostic picture differs from Europe and North America in an important way:

  • A distinct mutation spectrum. F508del — the dominant variant worldwide — is uncommon in Chinese patients. Different variants predominate, with p.G970D reported as the most frequent CFTR mutation in Chinese CF patients in published series. Imported commercial genotyping panels therefore perform poorly, and full CFTR sequencing is often necessary.
  • Misdiagnosis pathways. CF is frequently mistaken for idiopathic bronchiectasis, primary ciliary dyskinesia, or diffuse panbronchiolitis. Adults presenting with unexplained bronchiectasis plus sinus disease and pancreatic insufficiency are the group most often missed.
  • Later diagnosis. Without routine newborn screening, a substantial share of Chinese CF patients are diagnosed in adolescence or adulthood.

Clinical centres and registry

Peking Union Medical College Hospital (PUMCH), Beijing, is the central institution for CF in China. Its Department of Pulmonary and Critical Care Medicine, within the State Key Laboratory of Complex Severe and Rare Diseases, maintains the longest-running Chinese CF cohort and leads the national CF registry effort.

  • A National Registry on Chinese Patients With Cystic Fibrosis (NCT05289245) Led by PUMCH. Aims to establish prevalence, disease status, diagnosis and treatment patterns, quality of care, and outcomes for CF in China. Useful as a route to identify participating centres.
  • Cystic fibrosis: a rare disease emerging in China Overview of the current clinical picture of CF in China — a good orientation for clinicians unfamiliar with the local presentation.
  • Progression and mortality of patients with cystic fibrosis in China Longitudinal outcomes from the PUMCH cohort, published in the Orphanet Journal of Rare Diseases.
  • p.G970D is the most frequent CFTR mutation in Chinese patients with cystic fibrosis Directly relevant to why standard genetic panels miss CF in Chinese patients.

Global & general resources Worldwide

Reliable, freely available information in English. All of these are reputable organisations; none of them replace your own care team.

Care centres and patient organisations

  • Cystic Fibrosis Foundation (CFF) The most comprehensive CF resource in English. Includes the accredited care centre directory, clinical care guidelines, infection prevention guidance, and extensive patient and family material. US-focused, globally used.
  • Cystic Fibrosis Trust (UK) Strong practical guidance on daily life with CF, plus a helpline and UK-specific benefits and support information.
  • European Cystic Fibrosis Society (ECFS) Consensus clinical standards and the European CF Patient Registry. Aimed at clinicians rather than patients.

Understanding the condition

  • American Lung Association — Treating & Managing CF Clear overview of the components of CF treatment: airway clearance, inhaled therapies, CFTR modulators, and nutrition.
  • Cleveland Clinic — Cystic Fibrosis Overview Accessible summary of causes, symptoms, diagnosis, and treatment.
  • Mayo Clinic — Cystic Fibrosis Symptoms, causes, risk factors, and complications, with a companion diagnosis-and-treatment page.
  • NHLBI (US National Institutes of Health) — Cystic Fibrosis Government-authored, plain-language, and free of commercial interest.

Research literature

  • PubMed — cystic fibrosis Search the primary literature. Filter to "Free full text" to reach open-access papers via PMC.
  • ClinicalTrials.gov — cystic fibrosis studies Recruiting trials and registries, filterable by country. Useful for finding active CF programmes in a region.

Questions worth asking at your first specialist appointment

New diagnoses come with more information than anyone can absorb in one visit. Take this list with you.

  • Which CFTR variants do we have, and what does that mean for treatment options?
  • Are CFTR modulator therapies an option for us, and if not, why not?
  • What is our daily routine going to look like — which treatments, in what order, and how long will it take?
  • Which airway clearance device is right for us, and can someone watch us use it?
  • Is there pancreatic involvement, and do we need enzymes and vitamin supplements?
  • How often will we be seen, and who do we call between appointments?
  • What should make us call urgently rather than wait?
  • What infection-control precautions apply at home, at school, and at work?
  • Is there a dietitian, physiotherapist, and social worker on the team, and when do we meet them?
  • Should other family members be offered carrier testing?
